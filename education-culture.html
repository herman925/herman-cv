My educational philosophy is deeply rooted in my own cross-cultural journey. Growing up in international school environments (GSIS, Tanglin, Taipei British School) gave me a firsthand appreciation for diverse perspectives.

This experience fostered a deep cultural humility—a core counseling trait. It informs my work with minority families and my approach to inclusive education, ensuring that every learner feels seen, valued, and understood regardless of their background.

The route reads like a passport: stamped in Taipei, renewed in Singapore, settled in Hong Kong — first as a translator between languages at CUHK SCE, then between people: a psychologist at HKBU, and finally a guidance counsellor at CUHK.

Communicating the evidence

Coordinated media dissemination of survey findings and related qualitative evidence on preschool inclusion, school-level support and resources through the EdUHK–CNOPE joint press conference. Translated the English release, drafted the initial Q&A, checked wording against the research, and prepared reference-backed materials for media spokespeople. The work connected academic findings with questions about teacher training, interdisciplinary collaboration and support for children with special educational needs.

  • Incorporated academic and partner feedback into public-facing materials, including revisions to the English release.
  • Retained research limitations in answers to likely media questions and used references to support clear explanations of the findings.
  • Circulated clippings, photographs and interview records to academic and partner colleagues on 14 August for continued reference.
